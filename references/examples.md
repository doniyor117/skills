# Voice references

These examples are evidence, not templates. The first is directly supplied original writing; the next two are fuller samples recovered from the previous guide, with their editing history stated explicitly. Their labels matter: original writing and approved assistant drafts are different kinds of evidence. New drafts should fit the new task.

## 1. Doniyor’s own reply to screening questions

Source: supplied directly by Doniyor on 23 October 2026. This public excerpt keeps his original wording and mistakes, with bracketed redactions for test details, pay, and availability. The original private message is not included in this repository or its history. The study date describes what he wrote in that message; it does not settle other education dates.

> Good afternoon, glad to hear back!
>
> 1. I've been studying data science and analytics together since October 2025, so i have about a year or less of experience on both SQL and Python.
>
> 2. Currently i have still valid IELTS with [score omitted] overall score, and i do believe i have made a lit progress since then as curriculum and projects are mainly conducted in English.
>
> 3. Yes of course, [availability detail omitted].
>
> 4. I think starting from [amount omitted] is reasonable amount for me now.
>
> 5. [Day and time omitted] works well for me.
>
> Looking forward further updates, thanks!

What to notice: he answers in order, uses a warm opening, gives direct practical answers, and qualifies a judgement with “I think.” The message is not a cover letter. It does not need a project pitch, a personal philosophy, or a paragraph about his strengths.

What not to copy: lowercase “i,” accidental wording such as “a lit progress,” and missing articles are editing issues, not the voice. Do not infer a currency, salary range, current timetable, or available interview slot from the redactions. Do not reuse “about a year” in a later year without checking.

A possible light edit of the opening and closing, **illustrative and not separately approved**:

> Good afternoon, glad to hear back!
>
> [Direct answers to the questions.]
>
> Looking forward to further updates. Thanks!

The small scale of that edit is the point. It is not a replacement template for every reply.

Do not silently edit source examples to make them obey the guide.

## 7. Editorial contrasts

These are new illustrations of the guidance, not additional approved quotations.

**Overwritten:** “I am deeply honoured by your response and eager to demonstrate my suitability.”

**Closer to his reply:** “Good afternoon, glad to hear back!”

**Too absolute:** “Coaching beats removing.”

**More accurate:** “I recommended working with the watch group first, rather than relying on removals.”

**Overstated ownership:** “I wrote the BYD analysis.”

**Accurate scope:** “I calculated the ratios and wrote part of the analysis for a team assignment.”

**Forced branding:** “Every dataset tells a story, and I turn that story into impact.”

**Concrete explanation:** “I compared delivery delays with customer reviews.”

These alternatives are context-specific. Do not paste them into unrelated drafts, and do not mistake plain language for a rule against personality.
